Sushi Box Lids: How the Lid Line Works

17 October 2026 2 min read Shenzhen & Shantou, Guangdong

Our lid line in action — thermoforming, discharge, and the hand-checked packing bench.

A sushi box lid looks simple. It is not. It has to be clear enough to show off the food, rigid enough to survive stacking and shipping, and tight enough to actually close the tray. This is how ours are made, on our own line.

  1. Sheet inFood-grade PS or OPS sheet feeds in from a roll. Material choice sets clarity, stiffness and price.
  2. One-shot formingA multi-cavity tool presses the sheet into lids — every cavity in the tool produces one lid per cycle.
  3. DischargeFinished lids drop onto the conveyor and travel to packing.
  4. Checked by handEvery lid is visually checked and packed by hand before it is boxed for export.

All of it happens on our own equipment. No subcontracting, no relabeling someone else's stock as ours.

What this means for your order

Two things on that list change what you pay:

  • Cavity count. One press cycle makes as many lids as the tool has cavities. Higher count, lower unit price — which is why MOQ and price move together.
  • Standard or custom. Standard FP-00 to FP-11 lids use existing tools: no tooling charge, lower MOQ. A custom size or shape needs a new tool.

Our key products are tested by SGS against US FDA 21 CFR 177.1640 and EU Regulation (EC) No 1935/2004 with (EU) No 10/2011. Reports go out with the shipment, not after you chase them.
